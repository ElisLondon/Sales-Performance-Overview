## Client Background and Introduction

<img width="523" height="178" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/3196e52d-51e9-4786-81ca-9a04d5012c02" />

OfficeMax is a US based retailer specializing in technology products and office supplies for both consumer and corporate clients. Following its 2013 merger, the company faced challenges in expanding its customer base and driving sales growth.
Between 2015 and 2018, OfficeMax’s online channel generated **$2.2M in revenue across 10,000 customers and 5,000 orders**, providing a valuable dataset for performance evaluation. The data spans key dimensions including sales, product categories, customer segments, and regional demand.
This analysis reviews OfficeMax’s post-merger performance, uncovering trends and opportunities to strengthen sales and market positioning. The insights are organized around four **North Star Metrics** that capture the company’s growth drivers:
- **Sales trends** – revenue, order volume, and average order value (AOV)
- **Product performance** – category and sub-category analysis to guide marketing and product strategy
- **Segment analysis** – identifying growth drivers across customer segments
- **Regional results** – evaluating demand and product performance by region

Together, these findings provide actionable recommendations to enhance OfficeMax’s commercial performance and inform future strategy.


## Executive Summary
### Tracking Revenue Growth and Seasonal Peaks 2015 – 2018

<img width="956" height="607" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/5b468dd8-03ba-4a76-82af-a53485ff6a76" />


#### 1. Revenue Recovery & Seasonal Dependence
- Revenue **fell 5%** in 2016, but rebounded with **26% growth** in 2017 and **19% growth** in 2018
- **Q4** consistently delivers the strongest results, contributing **39% of annual revenue**, with Q1 and Q2 only responsible for **35% combined**
- **Declining Average Order Value** - AOV fell **15%** from **$246** in 2015 to **$209** in 2018, eroding margins despite rising order volumes.
#### 2. Product Performance & Category Dynamics
- Similar Category Revenue Generation: **Tech** generates slightly more revenue at **$803K** than **furniture ($708K)** and **office supplies ($684K)**.
- AOV and Order Count – **Tech ($443)** and **furniture ($341)** outperform office supplies ($116) in **AOV**; **Office supplies** produce **60% total orders**
- Sub-category imbalances – Both tech and furniture have sub-categories with **high AOV** and **high order count**.
#### 3. Customer Segments & Demand Stability
- Consumer stability – Consumers contribute **51% of revenue**, with consistent YoY spending over all categories
- Corporate & home office volatility – Corporate and home office **vary** notably YoY; tech produces **43%** of home office revenue.
#### 4. Regional Growth & Weakness
- West & East as growth engines – West revenue grew **60%** from **$146K** in 2015 to **$234K** in 2018 and East revenue rose **55%** from **$128K** to **$198K**.
- South underperforming – South revenue contributed just **17%** in total and only grew **12%**
  

### Executive Priorities
1.	**Defend Profitability through AOV uplift**: Counter declining AOV with upsell, bundling, and premium product campaigns.
2.	**Exploit Q4 peaks, mitigate Q1/Q2 weakness**: Optimize inventory and campaigns around Q4 peaks, launch retention offers in Jan-Feb to reduce seasonal drop off by 15%
3.	**Drive growth via repeat purchases and bundles**: Drive repeat purchases in office supplies; leverage technology/furniture for high value transactions, using bundles to increase order quantity
4.	**Optimise Consumer segment & Accelerate expansion in East & West**: Prioritize consumer strategies for reliable growth; Double down on West and East with tailored marketing and product expansion.

### Sales Revenue
1.	Sustained Growth Driven by **Holiday Season** – Revenue increased over time, with **Q4** consistently delivering the strongest gains.
2.	**2016 Decline** as Temporary Setback – Sales **fell 5%** in 2016, largely due to weaker **March and September** performance, before rebounding strongly in subsequent years.
3.	Seasonality: **Weak Q1**, stable mid year, **peak Q4**.
4.	**October Anomaly** – October revenue diverges from historical patterns, suggesting promotional or product-specific drivers worth investigating.

### Order Count
1.	**Order Growth Mirrors Revenue Trends** – **Higher order volumes in Q4** align with revenue growth, though increases are partly explained by rising AOV.
2.	October Discrepancy – Despite **consistently low order counts in October**, revenue rose in 2017–2018, indicating **purchases of higher-value items**.
3.	Early-Year Weakness – **Orders remain lowest in Jan–Feb**, reinforcing the seasonal demand challenge.
4.	Spring/Summer Recovery – Order counts steadily improve from March–August, showing gradual demand stabilization.
5.	2015 Outlier – March 2015 had unusually low orders despite high revenue, driven by exceptional AOV.

### AOV
1.	**High Volatility** Across Years – AOV fluctuates significantly with no consistent seasonal pattern.
2.	**March 2015 Spike** – Exceptional AOV explains anomalous revenue despite low order count.
3.	**Downward Trend** – AOV has steadily declined across the four years, suggesting margin pressure or increased reliance on lower value items.
4.	**October Upswing in Recent Years** – AOV in October 2017–2018 was significantly higher than in 2015–2016, highlighting the potential success of premium product sales or targeted promotions.

Low revenue months are primarily driven by **reduced order volumes**, which outweigh the impact of AOV. However, the **steady decline in AOV** signals a growing risk: even when customers do place orders, they are **spending less**. To counter this trend, marketing efforts should focus on **increasing basket size** and encouraging **higher spend per transaction**, ensuring that revenue growth is supported both by order frequency and order value.

### AOV
- Between 2015–2018, **technology** products delivered the **highest AOV** ($442.90), followed by **furniture** ($340.80) and **office supplies** ($115.80).
  - Align pricing and product mix in furniture and technology to sustain higher AOV levels.
- Technology and furniture consistently outperformed office supplies in AOV across all months.
  - Focus marketing on **upselling and bundling office supplies**, which consistently underperform in AOV
- Outliers in March 2015 ($1,541) and October 2017 ($1,051) inflated technology AOV.
  - **Investigate anomalies** to understand drivers and replicate successful strategies
- AOV ranges highlight category differences:
  - **Office Supplies**: $35 (lowest) – $214 (highest)
  - **Furniture**: $194 – $773
  - **Technology**: $201 – $1,541

### Order Count
- Order volume tells the opposite story: **office supplies consistently generated the highest number of orders**, outperforming both technology and furniture.
- Total orders: Office supplies (5,909), Furniture (2,078), Technology (1,813).
  - Maintain strong office supply order volumes by **reinforcing repeat purchase incentives** (subscriptions, loyalty programs
- Technology and furniture followed similar order count patterns, with September 2017 as the only major divergence (technology orders 44% lower).
  - Explore strategies to **boost technology and furniture order counts** (e.g., targeted campaigns, product bundles).
- Despite category differences, order counts across all three categories reflected the same **broader seasonal sales trends**.
The best approach would be to **balance strategies between order count and AOV** with **office supplies** being prioritized for **volume** and **technology/furniture** for **high value transactions**.

## Recommendations

### Sales Team
- **Seasonality management**: Smooth early year dips (Jan–Feb) with targeted offers, subscriptions, or loyalty incentives.
  - Revenue in Jan and Feb was 4% and 3% of the total between 2015 and 2018
- **Upsell opportunities**: Train sales teams to bundle high volume with high AOV products.
- **Focus on regional underperformance**: Reassess sales strategies in the South (pricing, distribution, customer engagement) to close the gap.
- **Leverage growth regions**: Double down on West and East where momentum is strongest—expand coverage and incentivize reps to capture demand.

### Marketing Team
- **Holiday campaigns**: Prioritize Q4 promotions, aligning messaging with peak demand periods.
- **October premium push**: Replicate successful October promotions across other months to lift AOV.
- **Segment targeting**:
  - Consumers → broad campaigns (consistent even demand).
  - Home office → technology-focused campaigns (43% skew).
  - Corporate → flexible campaigns to manage volatility.
- **Product bundling**: Promote bundles combining high order count items (e.g. binders) with premium products (e.g. machines/copiers) to maximize basket size.

### Product Team
- Category strategy:
  - **Technology** → sustain high AOV and expand product lines.
  - **Furniture** → maintain mid range AOV, explore premium upsell opportunities.
  - **Office supplies** → innovate bundles/subscriptions to lift value.
- **Sub category focus**: Prioritize top performers (phones, chairs, storage/binders) while phasing out non contributors (e.g., art/fasteners).
- **Investigate anomalies**: Analyse anomalous March and October spikes to identify product drivers and replicate success.
- **Inventory planning**: Align stock levels with seasonal peaks.

### Finance Team
- **Margin protection**: Address declining AOV by modelling impact on profitability; support upsell/bundle initiatives.
  - AOV fell 15% overall from 2015 to 2018
- **Resource allocation**: Channel investment toward high growth regions (West/East) and high AOV categories (tech/furniture).
- **Risk monitoring**: Track volatility in corporate and home office segments; build scenario models for revenue swings.
- **Performance measurement**: Implement KPIs that balance volume (orders) and value (AOV) to ensure sustainable growth.
